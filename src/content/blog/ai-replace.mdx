Relying heavily on AI tools provides a productivity boost and speed unmatched by
anything before. Or is it?

That sentence used to be challenged by many, who argued that the productivity
boost and extra velocity from AI-generated code translate into
an amalgamation of all sorts of issues, such as:
* low quality
* "bad taste" code
* security vulnerabilities
* poor architectural decisions

Nevertheless, most of us have concluded we need to integrate it even more into our
workflows, either because of
<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_of_missing_out">FOMO</a>
(fear of missing out) or because we actually managed to become more productive
with it, to a point where delegating more just seems the "sensible" decision to
make. But this begs the question.

<H2 title="What should we delegate to AI?" />
Is there a clear line where integrating AI into our workflows has diminishing
returns? Either in terms of productivity or skill formation?

One could argue that the more we delegate to AI, the more we can focus on other aspects of our work,
such as problem-solving, creativity, and strategic planning.

But is this accurate?

Is it possible to automate the entire development/thinking process? And most importantly, would this benefit us in the long run?

The answer to these questions is highly subjective and, in my opinion, depends on these variables:
* **Complexity**: how hard/what's at stake technically (bank vs button color)
* **Goals**: learn vs ship
* **Severity of the software failing**: e.g airplane goes swimming
* **Novelty**: how unprecedented the problem is not recombined training data

For example, if you are in the aviation business, I highly doubt you'll see
AI-generated code written anytime soon. Nor do I expect my bank to stop reading
code because they can "ship features faster". At least I hope so.. <Image src={sadCat} alt="sad cat" style={"width: 30px;display: inline;margin-top: -6px"} />

However, when stakes and overall impact are lower, it's just fine. In fact, I'd
argue the more you integrate AI to accelerate your development, the more net
positive it becomes, even if it results in more issues overall!
Having your internal dashboard break, or your SaaS product that affects 4
people going offline, is not a huge issue, nor will it require hotfixing at 2 AM.
Of course, again this is highly subjective and context dependent.

<H2 title="Skill formation" />
But the conversation shifts rapidly when we start talking about skill formation.
A very common statement among developers is:

`I was fortunate enough to start coding before AI.`

I couldn't agree more. Writing tons of code, solving and struggling with tough
algorithms, juggling 20-40 files of context, or having your PR nitpicked left
and right over minor details and code style, is what transforms you into a
better developer. Most importantly, it gives you the ability to understand code
and its tradeoffs. You stop treating systems as black boxes and can get a rough
idea of the techniques behind certain parts of them.

Nowadays, however, that big easy button is always there, more tempting than ever
to press. Writing code feels slow and painful, and our brains are wired to avoid
it at every chance they get.

To make matters worse, the <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills">research</a>
so far suggests that aggressively delegating your coding tasks to AI impacts your skill formation.

And it's not surprising, the absence of struggle when learning something new
**always** hinders progression. If I want to improve at understanding code,
**not** writing it myself is the best way to slowly fade into obscurity.

<H2 title="Not all coding tasks are the same" />
To avoid the fear-mongering, not all coding tasks are the same.
If you're re-styling that blue button to red, or you made another
CRUD app for the 100th time, you can safely delegate it to AI, you're not
missing out. In fact AI may even do a better job in such cases, since the examples are countless out there.

However, if you haven't built a CRUD app before and you need to learn how,
using AI to **specifically write the code for you** is doing yourself a
disservice. Long term, you're not going fast you're just wasting fuel.

<H2 title="But we'll just delegate everything to AI anyway.." />

You might think: if we're delegating all our code, technical decisions, and debugging to AI anyway, why bother learning "low-level" skills? Two reasons:

<H4 title="Value" />
- If AI does everything, what's the value you bring?

You have to answer this very concretely. If you can't, the market will render
you obsolete. You'll be easely replaced by a PO who can prompt better than you, because
they understand the domain and the problem space better. You're not a developer
anymore, you're a prompt engineer.

<H4 title="Guidance" />
- A deeper understanding of how things work lets you steer the AI more effectively.

I've lost count of how often a more junior colleague, and not only juniors, got stuck with the same models I or someone else use.
They'd paste huge walls of text, **semi-convincingly** explaining the root cause of our issue, only to be completely wrong.
Then someone would jump in with half a sentence and pinpoint the exact problem. Same tools, different results.
The difference was knowing where to start looking, that's experience and domain knowledge gained from struggling countless times, not model choice alone.

<H2 title="Conclusion" />

LLMs have forever changed the way we develop and even treat code. But it's still
a tool, a very powerful and fancy one if used correctly, but a tool nonetheless.

Also it's still very early to draw a consensus on how these tools impacts us in the
long term, but the signs we already have don't look good.. So I wouldn't use AI
as a replacement for learning and understanding code.
If you deprive yourself of that struggle, your abilities could fade, or at the very least
won't be aligned with being much of a developer anymore. You may find either yourself replaced, or needing to redefine your role as a whole.
Use AI wisely, and don't let it replace the very skills that make you valuable in the first place.
